## Core idea

A feedback loop occurs when the result of a change returns to influence the process that created it. A **positive feedback** amplifies the original change. A **negative feedback** reduces it.

## Worked example: ice and albedo

1. Rising temperature melts some bright sea ice.
2. Darker ocean water is exposed.
3. Dark water absorbs more incoming sunlight than bright ice.
4. The added heat raises local temperature and can melt more ice.

The loop amplifies the initial warming. “Positive” describes the direction of the feedback; it does not mean the outcome is beneficial.
